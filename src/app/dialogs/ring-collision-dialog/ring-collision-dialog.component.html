What does this mean?

These aren't real physics events. The stellar forge is programmed to prevent collisions, but a few slip through. When two objects are on a collision course, they simply pass straight through each other and carry on in their orbits.

Contact is measured along the line joining the two bodies, where a flat ring can actually meet another. Each object occupies a radial band there (a ring between its inner and outer edge; a solid body from its centre to its surface), so they touch while the separation lies between the sum of the two inner edges and the sum of the two outer ones.

That lower edge is why a close pass can register twice: the objects meet on the way in, then one passes inside the other's central hole — genuinely no longer touching — before meeting again on the way out. When that happens, "closest approach" isn't itself a collision; each of the two windows has its own deepest point instead, pinned to the edge of the band nearest closest approach. Ring tilt is not modelled (the data gives a tilt magnitude but not its direction), so exact durations are approximate.
